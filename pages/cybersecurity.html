Common Cybersecurity Threats for Students

Key digital risks that frequently target university students on and off campus.

Phishing Attacks

📧

Deceptive emails masquerading as campus IT, financial aid, or professors to steal login credentials and personal data.

Password Theft

🔒

Reusing passwords across personal and university accounts allows attackers to compromise your portal after third-party breaches.

Fake Login Pages

🌐

Spoofed CSUN SSO or banking login portals designed to harvest your username, password, and multi-factor authentication codes.

Public Wi-Fi Risks

📶

Unsecured or rogue Wi-Fi networks in cafes and public spaces where attackers can intercept unencrypted browsing traffic.

Malware & Ransomware

⚠️

Infected downloads, pirated textbooks, or malicious email attachments that lock files or install spyware on your device.

Social Engineering

👥

Manipulative messages or urgent phone calls exploiting trust and fear to trick students into sending money or sensitive details.

What To Do If You Get Hacked

Follow these five steps immediately if you believe your account or device has been compromised.

1. Contain the Threat

Disconnect from Wi-Fi if a device is infected, and sign out of all active sessions in your account security settings.

2. Secure Credentials

Change your CSUN and personal passwords from a clean device, and verify your multi-factor authentication settings.

3. Report to Campus IT

Contact the CSUN IT Help Center right away so they can lock unauthorized portal access and protect your records.

4. Scan & Remediate

Run a full antivirus scan on your computer and remove any unfamiliar browser extensions or third-party app permissions.

5. Monitor & Recover

Check your sent emails, financial aid direct deposit info, and enrolled classes for unauthorized changes.

⚠️
Report Suspicious Activity Immediately: Forward phishing emails as an attachment to abuse@csun.edu or contact the CSUN IT Help Center.
